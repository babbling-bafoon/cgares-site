Activation

CGARES provides volunteer amateur radio communications when normal systems are overloaded, damaged, or unavailable. This page covers how agencies request support and what members should do when readiness elevates.

Requesting CGARES Support (Agencies / EMA)

If you are with Cerro Gordo County Emergency Management, public safety, or another organization that may need communications support, contact the Emergency Coordinator directly:

  • Justin Bonjour, N0JBE — Emergency Coordinator
  • Email: ec@cgares.com
  • On air: primary repeater 147.15 MHz (+600 kHz, TSQ 103.5 Hz)

Please include who is requesting support, the nature of the incident or event, the communications need, preferred contact method, and any timing constraints. CGARES will respond as capacity and safety allow. We do not currently have formal memoranda of understanding (MOUs) on file; coordination is handled directly with the EC.

Echo Level vs. a Net Running

Echo level is separate from whether a net is running. A Skywarn, resource, or traffic net can be active at any Echo level. A severe thunderstorm or tornado warning for the county does not automatically raise Echo — we often remain at Echo 3 during most warnings. Full Echo definitions are in the CGARES SOP and summarized on the home page.

Member Steps When Echo Elevates

When conditions or the EC indicate elevated readiness, members should:

  1. Monitor primary voice channels — keep the radio on the main repeater (147.15 MHz, +600 kHz, TSQ 103.5 Hz) while awake; know the simplex fallback (147.570 MHz) if the repeater fails.
  2. Follow Echo actions from the SOP — for example, at Echo 4 announce monitoring periodically and check Winlink daily; at Echo 3 keep continuous monitoring while awake and be ready if called by name. At Echo 2, expect a Resource Net and short-notice deploy readiness.
  3. Check email — watch ec@cgares.com traffic and any instructions the EC sends.
  4. Wait for net or deployment direction — do not self-deploy. Respond when Net Control or the EC calls for check-ins, a Resource Net, or a specific assignment.
  5. Keep go-kit and power ready — batteries charged, portable/mobile capability as practical.

Frequencies, Echo actions, STATREP, and Resource Net procedures are detailed in the Standard Operating Procedures. Weekly net times are on the Nets & Training page.
